\subsection*{Research Questions}
\label{sec:rqs}

This study is guided by three core research questions:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{RQ1:} Can psychologically grounded behavioural features—
    including inactivity duration, active learning days, assessment
    consistency, and engagement trajectory—improve disengagement risk
    prediction beyond conventional academic metrics?

  \item \textbf{RQ2:} Can unsupervised algorithmic archetype (persona) modelling, derived from
    behavioural clustering, improve the specificity and relevance of
    personalised intervention recommendations in AI-assisted mentoring
    systems?

  \item \textbf{RQ3:} Does a multimodal integration of behavioural
    analytics, explainable AI (SHAP), and large-language-model-based
    mentoring outperform single-modality or behaviour-only approaches
    in terms of predictive accuracy, fairness, and deployment
    readiness?
\end{itemize}

\begin{abstract}
Student disengagement in online learning environments is a critical
determinant of academic failure, yet most early-warning systems rely on
shallow, non-interpretable features. We present \textit{EduGuard AI}, a
multimodal machine-learning framework that integrates psychologically
grounded behavioural analytics, explainable AI, and retrieval-augmented
large-language-model mentoring to detect disengagement risk and deliver
personalised interventions in real time.

Trained on the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset (OULAD,
\(n = 32{,}593\) students, 7 behavioural features), EduGuard AI employs
XGBoost as its core risk classifier, achieving a ROC-AUC of
\textbf{0.9486} (Accuracy 0.8891, F1 0.8884), and surpasses
Logistic Regression by \(+1.14\) percentage points in AUC
(\(\Delta\text{AUC} = 0.0114\), DeLong \(z = 2.857\),
\(p = 0.0043\)).
Five-fold stratified cross-validation confirms stability
(AUC \(= 0.9474 \pm 0.0028\)).
SHAP analysis identifies inactivity duration (\(\bar{|\phi|}=1.373\)),
active learning days (\(\bar{|\phi|}=1.067\)), and mean assessment
score (\(\bar{|\phi|}=0.949\)) as the primary predictors of
disengagement.
K-Means persona clustering (\(k=6\), Silhouette
\(= 0.326 \pm 0.001\) across 20 seeds, ARI stability
\(= 0.971 \pm 0.035\)) identifies six unsupervised algorithmic archetypes
(personas) grounded in behavioral theory, enabling targeted mentoring via a RAG-based LLM pipeline.
Fairness evaluation across gender, disability, region, and
socioeconomic status (IMD band) confirms consistent performance
(AUC range: \(0.934\)–\(0.962\)).
Robustness testing at 20\% feature noise yields AUC \(= 0.928\),
confirming deployment resilience.
Inference latency for a single prediction is \(0.47\)~ms (memory footprint
181~MB, no GPU required), supporting high-throughput concurrent real-time use.
EduGuard AI provides a complete, deployable, and ethically evaluated
framework for AI-driven student support in higher-education online
learning.
\end{abstract}

    re-engagement schedule, assessment scaffold provided.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

While EduGuard AI demonstrates strong performance across multiple
evaluation dimensions, several important limitations must be
acknowledged.

\textbf{(1) Cross-Dataset Generalisation.}
All experiments are conducted exclusively on the OULAD dataset from
the Open University, UK. While OULAD is a widely used benchmark in
learning analytics research, it reflects a specific cultural, pedagogical,
and technological context. Performance may degrade when applied to
institutions with different virtual learning environments, assessment
structures, or student demographics. Evaluation on EdNet \cite{choi2020ednet},
MOOC datasets \cite{kizilcec2013deconstructing}, or proprietary
institutional datasets is necessary before broader deployment claims.

\textbf{(2) No Longitudinal Intervention Validation.}
EduGuard AI predicts disengagement risk and generates intervention
recommendations, but no longitudinal randomised controlled trial has
been conducted to validate whether the recommended interventions
causally reduce dropout or improve outcomes. The system demonstrates
predictive validity, but intervention efficacy remains unvalidated.

\textbf{(3) Persona Drift and Validation.}
K-Means persona assignments are computed statically from historical
data. In real deployments, student behaviour evolves dynamically within
a semester. The current implementation does not support online persona
re-assignment, meaning a student who recovers from a burnout pattern
may retain an outdated persona label throughout the course. Furthermore,
these personas are purely unsupervised algorithmic archetypes based on
behavioral features; while theoretically grounded, they have \textbf{not}
been clinically validated by psychologists or domain experts.

\textbf{(4) LLM Hallucination Risk.}
The AI Mentor component uses a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
pipeline with a local large language model (Ollama). Despite RAG
grounding, LLMs can generate plausible-sounding but factually incorrect
advice, particularly in edge cases not well-represented in the
knowledge base. All LLM-generated interventions should be reviewed by
qualified educators before delivery to students.

\textbf{(5) Dataset Bias and Label Definition.}
Disengagement risk is defined as final result $\in$ \{Withdrawn, Fail\},
which is a proxy for disengagement rather than a direct measurement.
Students may withdraw for reasons unrelated to engagement (e.g.,
personal circumstances, employment). The model may therefore penalise
students who choose to withdraw for legitimate reasons.

\textbf{(6) Ethical Deployment Considerations.}
Algorithmic risk scores can stigmatise students if communicated without
appropriate safeguards. High-risk labels must never be exposed directly
to students. All risk predictions should inform educator support
strategies, not automated gatekeeping decisions. Informed consent,
data minimisation, and compliance with GDPR and institutional ethics
boards are mandatory prerequisites for deployment.

\textbf{(7) Generalisability of Cognitive Features.}
The deep learning models (LSTM, GRU, TCN) are trained on proxy
cognitive-load sequences derived from OULAD assessment patterns.
True multimodal cognitive data (e.g., eye-tracking, facial action units,
physiological signals) was not available. The deep learning results
therefore represent a lower bound on potential multimodal performance.

\textbf{Future Work.}
Priorities include: (i) cross-institution validation; (ii) online
learning persona tracking; (iii) human evaluation of AI mentor
interventions with real students; (iv) integration of authentic
physiological and video-based cognitive signals; (v) causal inference
methods to evaluate intervention effectiveness.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented EduGuard AI, a multimodal machine-learning framework
for early disengagement risk detection and personalised AI mentoring
in online higher-education environments. Built on the Open University
Learning Analytics Dataset ($n = 32{,}593$ students), the system
combines psychologically grounded behavioural feature engineering,
gradient-boosted classification, SHAP explainability, unsupervised
persona clustering, and retrieval-augmented LLM-based intervention
generation into a coherent, deployable pipeline.

The proposed XGBoost classifier achieves ROC-AUC $= 0.9486$, Accuracy
$= 0.8891$, Precision $= 0.9474$, Recall $= 0.8364$, and F1 $= 0.8884$
on the held-out test set, significantly outperforming Logistic Regression
(DeLong $z = 2.857$, $p = 0.0043$).
Five-fold cross-validation confirms robustness (AUC $= 0.9474 \pm 0.0028$).
SHAP analysis identifies inactivity duration, active learning days, and
assessment consistency as the primary psychological risk signals, providing
educators with transparent, feature-level explanations for each prediction.

K-Means clustering ($k=6$) with 20-seed stability validation
(ARI $= 0.971 \pm 0.035$) reveals six unsupervised algorithmic archetypes
(personas) — Silent Isolator, Burnout Pattern, Anxiety-Spike Learner,
Passive Watcher, Consistent Learner, and Last-Minute Survivor —
enabling targeted, persona-specific mentoring interventions delivered
through the RAG-LLM pipeline.

Comprehensive fairness evaluation demonstrates consistent performance
across gender ($\Delta\mathrm{AUC} = 0.0022$), disability status
($\Delta\mathrm{AUC} = 0.0026$), and socioeconomic band
($\Delta\mathrm{AUC} = 0.0269$, all groups above AUC $= 0.930$).
Robustness testing confirms AUC $= 0.928$ under 20\% feature noise.
Inference latency of $0.47$~ms per prediction (memory 181~MB, no GPU)
confirms real-time deployment readiness without GPU infrastructure.

EduGuard AI represents a significant step toward evidence-based,
ethically grounded, and practically deployable AI systems for student
success in online learning. The complete pipeline — from raw OULAD data
ingestion through FastAPI deployment and React front-end visualisation —
is fully reproducible from the repository. Future work will focus on
cross-institutional validation, longitudinal intervention efficacy
studies, and integration of physiological multimodal signals.
